01 · Scene Setting

$124 trillion is moving. The investor is changing.

By 2048, $124T will move from one generation to the next. As the greatest wealth transfer in history unfolds, a new investor is emerging who is fundamentally different. 70% of inherited wealth will be controlled by women (BofA), and the next-gen investor has a different, impact-focused set of priorities.

These new preferences are rewriting the demands of wealth management. Underlying behaviors for millennials and Gen Z are shifting from traditional to deliberate consumption, from delegated decisions to ownership, and from mass consumption to personalized, high-quality, data-informed insights and recommendations.

The next generation is firing their parents' advisors at a rate of roughly 70% because of a needs gap: they are skeptical of the 'traditional' portfolio, and want their capital to reflect their personal identity and values. The financial industry can't service these needs until they begin to understand them.

02 · The Gap

Existing tools to capture values flatten human preferences to SDGs and exclusion lists.

But human values are much more nuanced, informed by lived experiences. The Investment Policy Statement is often a legally load-bearing document, and by limiting the language through which sentiment can be expressed, we limit how money moves.

Today's tools

The static survey.

  • Static checkboxes against SDGs and ESG indicators
  • Exclusion-list theater: what you are against, never what you are for
  • One pass at onboarding, then frozen forever
  • No vocabulary for power, agency, or lived stakes
  • Reductive enough to be ignored, not predictive of behavior
03 · Solution

We are designing a more human onboarding.

Most values-based investing tools ask what you care about investing in. We ask first about you, and use the answer to surface your identity as an investor.

Money is one of the most expressive things a human deploys. We already use voting, volunteering, and consumption to make our values legible. Capital should not be the exception.

The thesis is simple. By creating a dynamic data foundation for understanding the preferences behind the investor (not the financial actor), we make personalized, algorithmic matching possible for the first time. With like-minded peers. With investment opportunities. With reporting that speaks back in the investor's own voice.

04 · The Tool

A values codification experience, designed to be worth the time.

An AI-led conversation that probes for implicit values, digs deeper into emotional points, uncovers what "enough" looks like, and uses forced-choice tradeoffs to reveal what stated preferences cannot. The output is structured enough to drive an IPS, investment decisions, and reporting.

  • i.

    Questions as invitations to reflect

    Not data extraction. Each prompt is calibrated to feel like the kind of question a thoughtful friend would ask. Not an intake form.

  • ii.

    Adaptive to literacy

    The model meets the investor where they are. A first-time philanthropist and a seasoned LP receive different vocabulary, different framings, different depths.

  • iii.

    Latent over stated

    We never ask "what do you care about." We probe behavior, surface contradictions, and triangulate forced-choice tradeoffs to reveal values stated preferences cannot.

  • iv.

    Emotionally attuned

    The model recognizes when something is heated. Heat is signal. It slows down, deepens, and treats the moment as the heart of the data. Not noise.

  • v.

    A living document

    The output is not a frozen IPS. It is a profile that evolves with every swipe, conversation, and decline. Editable, shareable with a partner or advisor.

  • vi.

    Built to say no faster

    The most underrated function of values codification: helping investors decline misaligned opportunities with confidence, and re-deploy attention to ones that fit.
